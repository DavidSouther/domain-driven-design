# Shopping and Recommendation Links

When research produces a recommendation for a product, service, book, library, or other offering, the recommendation carries direct links to the source. A recommendation without a link is half a recommendation.

## Linking Rules

- **Always include a direct link** to the recommended item's page on the offerer's own site.
- **If the offerer's site does not sell directly,** add a second link to a retailer who does, with a one-sentence note that the offerer is not a direct seller.
- **If no direct page exists,** link as close to the item as the offerer's site allows (a category page, a product line page).
- **Prefer canonical sources** over aggregators or affiliate-laden retailer pages. The order of preference is: offerer's own site, official marketplace (App Store, npm, PyPI, crates.io), major retailer, smaller retailer.
- **Include an image** of the product when one is available. Prefer the image hosted by the offerer over a third-party photo. Images render well on a phone, where most of these recommendations are read, so a recommendation that has one is poorer without it.
- **Link inline, in the prose.** The product name is the link. Follow the inline style in [citations.md](citations.md); a chat recommendation does not carry a footnote list.

## Comments and Reviews

Deliberately include products and reviews in your search, but do not take their results at face value. Look for specific evidence about the product or service provided. Generally weight feedback based on how closely the reviewer's background seems to align with the user's.

## Non-Retail Recommendations

The same rules apply to non-retail recommendations. For an open-source library, link the landing page, the docs, and the source repository. For a paper, link the publisher's page and the open-access PDF if one exists. For a tool with no obvious "buy" surface, link the page closest to installation or first use.
